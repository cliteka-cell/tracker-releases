Getting started in ten minutes

Install Tracker, make your first tracker, log some time, set a goal, write a note, add a book and keep your data safe. Each step is short. When you want every detail, the full manual has them.

  1. Install it

    Download for Windows

    Open the file you downloaded. If Windows says “Windows protected your PC”, choose More info and then Run anyway (the program is not signed with a paid certificate). Click Next and Install; it needs no administrator rights. When it finishes, Tracker opens in your web browser, and a small Tracker icon appears near the clock. Click that icon to open Tracker again later; right-click it to quit.

    You can also start it from the Start menu. Tracker only answers to your own computer: nobody else can open it, and it cannot be opened from a phone.

  2. Make your first tracker

    The first time, Tracker shows a welcome page. Pick the closest starting point: a language I am learning, my studies (courses or a degree), research or a thesis, or something else. Give your tracker a name, say what each part of it is called (a Course, a Language, a Topic), and if you know them, type your first ones separated by commas. Then press Create my tracker. You can change all of it later in Settings.

  3. Log your first session

    On the Dashboard, choose a course (or language) and an activity, then press Start. The timer runs even if you close the tab; Tracker finds it again when you come back. Pause stops the clock without ending the session, and Stop & save saves it. Started late? Started earlier? moves the start back by 5, 10 or 15 minutes or more. Repeat last starts the same course and activity as your last session in one click.

    Forgot to start the timer? Open Log and use Add a past session: pick the course, the activity, the date, the time and the minutes.

  4. Set a goal

    Open Settings. Each course has a Weekly goal in hours (it shows on the Dashboard as a progress bar) and an optional Total goal with a finish date; Tracker then tells you how many hours a week it takes to get there. Further down, This tracker has a Daily goal, which the Today card on the Dashboard measures your day against.

  5. Write a note that links to others

    Open Notes and click New note. Select text and use the toolbar to format it, or paste a screenshot straight in. Tie the note to one or more of your courses, and press Ctrl+Enter to save. To link to another note, type its title between double brackets, like [[Sampling]]. Each note then shows which notes link to it. Switch from Cards to Graph to see how everything connects.

  6. Add a book

    Open Library and press Add. Type an ISBN to find one exact book, a DOI to find an article, or some words to search Open Library (books) and Crossref (articles). Check the details and save. If nothing is found, press Enter it by hand instead. Then set its status (To read, Reading, Paused, Read), and type the page you are on in Now on page; Tracker works out your pace and how long is left. Pick the book in the timer, and the time you spend counts towards it.

  7. Watch your progress

    Stats shows daily study time (7 days up to a year), split by course if you like, how this week compares with last week, and the totals by course and by activity. Overview (top of the sidebar) combines every tracker you have.

  8. Keep your data safe

    Tracker saves a copy of your data automatically once a day and keeps the last 14. In Overview, Settings, Data and backups you can press Back up now, put any saved copy back with Restore, bring in a file from anywhere on your computer with Import from a file (it shows what is in the copy first, and keeps what you have now so the restore can be undone), and download everything as one file, as a zip of readable data, or your sessions as a spreadsheet. The saved copies are on the same computer as your data, so now and then copy that folder somewhere else.
