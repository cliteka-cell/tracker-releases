1. How Tracker is organised

Tracker is a program that runs on your own Windows PC or Mac and shows its pages in your web browser. Nothing is stored online: there is no account and no server of ours. The pieces fit together like this:

PieceWhat it is
TrackerOne area of your life or studies, for example “Japanese” or “My degree”. You can have as many as you like; each has its own pages, courses, activities, notes and library. They are listed in the sidebar.
SymbolThe small icon of a tracker, picked from a set (there are no emoji). It is shown in the sidebar and on the Overview.
CourseA part of a tracker: a course, a language, a topic. You choose the word (Course, Skill, Language, Topic...). It also appears as a “subfield” in some places.
ActivityWhat you did: Reading, Lecture, Practice, Listening, Writing...
SessionOne stretch of time: a course, an activity, a start, a length, and optionally a note and a book.
NoteSomething you wrote, tied to courses and books, that can link to other notes.
Book or articleA text in your Library that you read and can attach sessions and notes to.
DocumentSomething you write in Library, Writing: an essay, an article, a chapter.
GoalA weekly or total number of hours for a course, or a daily goal for the tracker.

Each tracker has six pages along the top: Dashboard, Log, Notes, Library (Books and Writing), Stats and Settings, which holds the settings of that tracker (its courses, activities, name, symbol and goals) together with the theme, the books settings and the notes graph settings, which are used in every tracker and are the same in every browser you use. Overview, at the top of the sidebar, combines all your trackers and has two pages of its own: Overview and Settings, which holds the settings of the whole program (data and backups, book search, the AI assistant, your phone, privacy and about). The Archive link under it holds every book of every tracker, and books in no tracker. In this manual, “App settings, Privacy” means the Privacy section of that second page.

2. Installing, starting, quitting

Installing

Download Tracker-Setup.exe and open it. Windows may say “Windows protected your PC” because the program is not signed with a paid certificate: choose More info, then Run anyway. Setup installs for your user only and needs no administrator rights. It offers a Desktop shortcut and adds Tracker to the Start menu. Tracker works on Windows 10 and 11 (64-bit), and on macOS 13.5 or newer (below); there is no Linux version yet.

Install on a Mac

Download Tracker-Mac.dmg (one download for Apple Silicon and Intel Macs, macOS 13.5 or newer), open it, and drag Tracker onto the Applications folder. Tracker is not signed with a paid Apple certificate, so macOS stops it the first time, once:

  • macOS 15 (Sequoia) and newer: open Tracker, close the message, then open System Settings, Privacy & Security, scroll down to Security and press Open Anyway next to the line about Tracker, and confirm. The button is there for about an hour after the message.
  • macOS 13 and 14: Control-click (or right-click) Tracker in the Applications folder, choose Open, then Open again.

After that it opens like any app, and updates from inside Tracker do not ask again. On a Mac, Tracker’s icon is a small clock in the menu bar at the top right (it has no Dock icon); click it to open Tracker, Control-click it for its menu. Your data is in ~/Library/Application Support/Tracker/data and the log in ~/Library/Application Support/Tracker/logs/tracker.log. To remove it, quit Tracker and drag it to the Trash; the data folder stays until you delete it.

Starting

Start Tracker from the Start menu or the Desktop shortcut (on a Mac, from the Applications folder). It runs quietly in the background with no window of its own, opens in your default browser, and puts a small icon near the clock (if you cannot see it, click the little arrow next to the clock). Click the icon to open Tracker in the browser; right-click it for Open Tracker, Show the log folder and Quit Tracker. Starting Tracker a second time just opens the first one.

The address in the browser is http://localhost:3417 (or a nearby number if that one is busy). It works only on your computer: no other device on your network can open it, unless you turn on Use Tracker from your phone and pair a phone (see Your phone).

Quitting

Right-click the icon (Control-click on a Mac) and choose Quit Tracker, or press Quit Tracker in App settings, About. Closing the browser tab does not stop Tracker, and a running timer is kept (it lives in your browser, see The timer).

Removing it

On a Mac, drag Tracker to the Trash (see above). On Windows: Windows Settings, Apps, Installed apps, Tracker, Uninstall. It asks whether to delete your data as well; the default is No, so installing Tracker again later carries on where you left off.

3. Trackers

The first time you open Tracker you see the welcome page.

  • Start with your own tracker. Choose A language I am learning, My studies (courses or a degree), Research or a thesis or Something else. Each comes with sensible activities and a symbol you can change later. Then name it, say what each part is called (one word, like Course, Language or Topic) and optionally type your first ones separated by commas. Create my tracker makes it.
  • Try it with sample data. Adds a made-up semester: three courses, four weeks of sessions, nine linked notes (some written under others), two books and goals. A banner on every page says it is sample data and has Remove sample data, which takes only the sample away (a copy of your data is saved first).
  • I already have a copy of my data. Opens the restore and import options, for a copy or export from another computer (see Backups, exports, moving).

More trackers: press New tracker at the bottom of the sidebar. Changing one: its own Settings, This tracker, lets you change its symbol (an icon chosen from a set, which also shows in the sidebar and on the Overview) and name, what each part is called, its daily goal, and its order in the sidebar. Deleting one: Delete this tracker in the same place. Tracker shows what will be removed (sessions, courses, activities, notes and books), and if the tracker holds any sessions, notes or books it asks you to type the tracker’s name before Delete permanently works. A copy of your data is saved first (“Before a delete” in the list of saved copies).

4. Courses and activities

Both are managed in the tracker’s own Settings, in the lists at the top. You can add, rename, recolour and delete them, and drag the grip (or focus it and use the arrow keys) to put them in the order you like.

Courses

  • Weekly goal: hours per week. It becomes a progress bar on the Dashboard.
  • Total goal: hours in all, with an optional finish date. The Dashboard then shows how far along you are and, with a date, how many hours a week it takes to finish in time.
  • Passive: a passive course is left out of the Dashboard’s weekly goals list (for example something you only follow loosely). Its time and goals are kept.
  • The colour you pick is used in lists, charts, notes and the graph.
  • Each course has its own page: click its name wherever it appears (the Dashboard’s goals, a book’s course, a note’s course, Stats, or Open in Settings). It shows the course’s time this week and in all, its books with their progress, its notes, its documents and its latest sessions, each leading on to where it lives.

Activities

Each has a name, a colour and an icon (pick the icon from the pictures). Add the ones you actually use; an activity can be used with any course.

Deleting a course or activity asks in place what to do with what it has: Move them and delete moves its sessions (and for a course its notes, books and documents) to another one first; Delete them too deletes its sessions with it. A copy of your data is saved before anything is deleted. If you only want a course out of the way, mark it Passive instead.

5. The Dashboard

  • Timer: see the next section.
  • Reading now: the books you have marked as Reading, with the page you are on and an estimate of how long is left. Continue reading opens a book that has its file where you stopped.
  • Flashcards due: how many of your flashcards are due, with Review (shown only when some are).
  • Today: time logged today against the daily goal, your streak (days in a row with time logged, in any tracker) and a calendar of your last weeks. Each square is a day; the darker, the more time. Click a day to see that day’s sessions in the Log. The number of weeks shown depends on how wide the card is: squares are kept big enough to click.
  • Weekly goals: a bar for the whole tracker and one for each course that has a goal. A course’s name opens its page.
  • Long-term goals: courses with a total goal, with percent done, hours to go and the pace needed.
  • Recent sessions: your latest sessions, with the book and note if any.

6. The timer

Starting

Choose a course and an activity. If you keep a Library, a third box lets you pick the book or article you are reading (“Not reading anything in particular” otherwise); picking a book also selects its course, and a book with its file has Read it (Open the book while the timer runs). Press Start. Repeat last starts the same course, activity and book as your most recent session in one click.

While it runs

  • Pause stops the clock without ending the session; press it again to resume. Paused time is not counted.
  • You can type a note about the session while it runs.
  • Started earlier? moves the start back by 5m, 10m, 15m, 30m, 45m or 1h when you forgot to press Start. It also works before you start: the same buttons then start the timer now, counting from that long ago.
  • The timer keeps running if you close the tab, reload the page or quit and reopen your browser: it is kept in your browser, and its time is worked out from when it started. (If you clear your browser’s site data, a running timer is lost, so save first.)
  • The bell icon switches break reminders on or off. With it on, your browser may ask permission to show notifications, and Tracker reminds you to take a break after every 30 minutes of active time (paused time does not count). The choice is kept in this browser.

Saving

Stop & save stores the session. If saving fails (for example the disk is full), Tracker says why and the timer keeps running, so nothing is lost. Right after saving, a bar says “Saved” with the time and offers Add note (or Skip). If the session was about a book or article, it also asks Now on page so you can type the page you reached and press Save page.

7. The Log

  • Add a past session opens a form: choose the course, activity, optionally a book, the date, the time, the minutes and a note, then Add. The date and time start as now, or as the day you clicked in the Dashboard calendar.
  • Sessions lists everything newest first, with how many and how long in all. Point at a row for its buttons: write a note about that session (shown all the time when it has notes, with how many), edit it (every field can be changed) and delete it. A deleted session leaves at once; Undo brings it back for a few seconds.
  • A session’s book or document is a link to it, and each of its notes leads to the Notes tab and to its page in the book.
  • Show only one course, one activity or one book with the boxes above the list (Show all clears them). Coming from a day in the calendar, the list shows only that day. A book’s sessions in the Library and a course in Stats open the Log shown that way.

8. Stats

  • Daily study time as bars, for 7d, 14d, 30d, 90d or 1y. Split by course puts each course’s time side by side within a day, left to right in the order of your course list. The side says hours. Hover or focus a bar for the exact time.
  • This week vs last week: this week so far, last week up to the same day (with the difference in percent) and last week in total, with a chart by weekday.
  • By course and by activity: where the time went, as bars with totals. A course opens its page, an activity the Log showing only it.

9. Overview

The first item in the sidebar. It adds up all your trackers: today, this week, all time with the number of sessions, your streak (a day counts if any tracker has time on it), a daily chart, time per tracker, your top courses across trackers, and the most recent sessions.

10. Notes and the graph

Writing

  • Press New note to open the editor (it starts folded once you have notes, so they come first). Anything half written is kept if you leave, until you save or discard it.
  • Select text and use the toolbar: bold, italic, strikethrough, highlight, code, heading, quote, bullet list, checklist, link and picture. Ticking a checklist item on a saved note works right on the card.
  • Pictures: paste a screenshot, or use the picture button. They are stored inside your data (see Backups).
  • Start from: Learned, Question, Checklist and Terms fill in a starting shape.
  • Courses: tick one or several; the note then appears under each. About ties it to a book or article, with an optional page number (Now on p. 118 fills in the page the book is at). A thin line of each course's colour runs along the top of the card (a note in several courses shows every colour side by side) and curves with the card's corners in every theme.
  • Under: put the note under one or more other notes (type part of a title and pick it; the × takes it out again). See Notes under notes.
  • Pin keeps a note at the top. Ctrl+Enter saves (another key can be chosen in Shortcuts).
  • You can also write a note right after a session (see The timer) or about a session in the Log.

Linking

Type a note’s title between double brackets, like [[Sampling]], and it becomes a link. A link to a title that does not exist yet is shown as an “unwritten link”, ready to be written. Each card lists Linked from: the notes that point to it. Two notes can have the same title: the list that opens as you type [[ shows what tells them apart (the note each is under, its book, its subfields, the day it was made), and a link picked there, or made in the graph, remembers which one it means. While you edit it shows a short code after the title ([[Reasoning^05952bws]]); on the card it shows just the title. When a second note takes a title, the links that went to the first one keep going there.

Notes under notes

A note can sit under another note, for example Random sampling under Sampling. A note can be under several notes at once, and a note under a note can have notes under it too, as deep as you like. Put a note under another with Under in its editor, or with Put under... in the graph.

  • A note is in the courses of every note above it as well as in its own. In the example, Random sampling is in Research methods because Sampling is, without being given that course itself. In the note’s editor that course shows as a dashed chip. You can still give it a course of its own as well (press the chip), but a new note under a note, or about a book that is under its course, is not given the course you were filtering by or the one a running timer is for: it has it already, and a second line to the same place would only add clutter.
  • A note is also about the books of every note above it, the way it is in their courses. If Sampling is about a book, Random sampling shows that book too, as a dashed chip, and in the graph its bubble lists it under Also about. There is no line of its own to that book. A note can still be about a book of its own as well. Its editor shows the book too (the About box reads the book, through the note above), and the Page box is there as well: the page is then in that book, and it is shown beside the dashed chip.
  • Each card shows Under with the notes it is under, and how many notes are under it; click one to go to it.
  • A note cannot be put under itself, or under a note that is already under it. Tracker refuses that and says why.
  • Deleting a note that has notes under it keeps them: they are given its courses and move up to the notes it was under. The question before deleting says so.
  • A [[link]] and being under a note are separate: removing one leaves the other.
  • The faint dashed lines the AI suggests (when it is on) never join notes of one family: two notes under the same note, or one above the other. The hierarchy already ties those together.

Books under courses, notes under books

The same idea reaches the Library: a book can sit under the course it is about, and the notes you write about a book sit under that book. So a course holds its books, and a book holds its notes.

  • When you choose a course for a book in the Library, Tracker asks how the book is joined to it: Put it under the course (the first choice) or A normal connection, a plain line that says nothing more. Books you already had are under their course.
  • A note you write about a book (About in the note’s editor) is put under that book at once, and you do not have to choose.
  • As with notes under notes, a note is in the course of the book it is under, as well as its own courses. A book that is only connected to its course does not pass the course down to its notes.
  • In the graph, a book under its course and a note under its book are joined by dashed lines, each in its own look (see Four kinds of line for being under something below). Put under... works for them too: a book goes under a course, a note goes under a book. Selecting the line of a book and its course shows Type: Under or Normal, to switch between the two.

Grid, full text and list

Beside Cards and Graph, three small buttons choose how the cards show: Grid (small cards with the title, a few lines, the course dots, the book and the date; the usual choice), Full text (every note in full) and List (one row per note). S, M and L set the size in Grid (how many cards side by side and how many lines show) and in List (how roomy the rows are). In Grid and List, click a note to show it in full where it is, with its pictures and buttons; click its title again, press Esc or click another note to make it small again. A note opened from a link, a citation or the graph is shown in full. The same choice is in each tracker’s Settings, Notes, and it is kept in this browser for every tracker.

On a card

  • The book chip opens the book in the reader at this note (its highlight lit up, or its page); a book without its file opens its Library card. A course’s name opens the course’s page.
  • A note written about a highlight shows the highlighted words above it, leading to them in the book.
  • Make a flashcard (the cards button) makes a flashcard from the note: its title on the front and its text on the back, both changeable first.
  • Delete takes the note away at once, with Undo for a few seconds. After that it is in the Bin (the bin button next to the search) for 30 days: Put back brings it back with its courses, its place under other notes, the notes that were under it, its highlights, flashcards and pictures; Delete for good and Empty the bin delete it.

Finding

Filter by All, Pinned, a course, or No course, or type in Search notes. A course lists its notes and the notes under them. Notes can be dragged into your own order. The search and the filter are kept when you come back. A book’s notes in the Library opens the Notes tab showing only that book’s notes (the × on its chip shows them all again), with the book’s highlights that have no note folded above them, each with Open in the book and Make a flashcard.

Flashcards

Flashcards is the third view of the Notes tab, next to Cards and Graph (there in a tracker with no notes yet too), and needs no AI. Start reviewing fills the window with the cards that are due, one at a time, and nothing else: how far you are along the top, and Quit reviewing (or Esc) to go back. You answer Again, Hard, Good or Easy, and each button shows when the card would come back. Cards are scheduled with FSRS, the same method Anki uses: each card comes back when you would still remember it with the chance set under Settings (Remember: 80%, 85%, 90% or 95% of cards; higher means more reviews, 90% suits most). New cards a day (20 to begin with) is how many cards you have not seen yet are shown each day. Both are kept for each tracker, and every answer is kept with the card.

  • Decks. A card can be in a deck (type its name when you edit it; Chinese::HSK 1 is a deck inside a deck). The deck box above the list shows one deck, and the cards inside its decks, for reviewing, searching and exporting.
  • Suspend on a card leaves it out of reviews until you take it back. Search the cards finds cards by their words; Show more loads the rest of a long list.
  • Import from Anki brings in an Anki deck (.apkg) or a whole Anki collection (.colpkg, from Anki’s Export or its backups folder), from old and new versions of Anki: its decks, its cards (front and back as Anki shows them, cloze cards too, as text; pictures and sounds are left out), suspended cards, how far you are with each card (with FSRS memory when Anki had FSRS on) and the history of your answers. A card that is already here is skipped, so the same deck can be brought in again to add only what is new.
  • Export to Anki saves the cards (of the deck shown) as an .apkg file that Anki opens, with their decks and how far you are with each. Bringing that file back here later does not make the cards twice.

Add a card by hand with a front and a back, or make one from a note card, a highlight in the reader (its menu), a highlight without a note, or an AI answer. Each card says which note or book it came from, as a link. A deleted card can be taken back with Undo for a few seconds. With the AI on, it can also write cards from a note, a course or a book for you to check.

The graph

Press Graph (next to Cards) to see your notes as dots joined by their links. Courses are the large ring-shaped dots, books are rectangles, notes are dots coloured by course (a note in two courses is split in two colours).

  • The tick boxes Courses, Unwritten links and Books show or hide those kinds of dots; Fit fits everything in view. Your choices are remembered.
  • Click a dot or a line and its details open in a bubble beside it. A line’s bubble names its kind (the names used in Customise) and its two ends, with an arrow from what is under to what it is under. The bubble follows the dot when you move or zoom the picture, opens on the dot’s other side near the edge of the picture, and has the buttons Open note (or Open in Library for a book), Connect to... and Clear. Clicking empty space closes it.
  • The note panel. Open note in a note’s bubble (or a double-click on its dot) shows the note as its card in a panel at the right of the graph, below it on a narrow screen: its text, pictures (a picture of a page too), courses, book and links. A [[link]] on it shows the linked note in the panel. Edit under the card opens the whole editor in the same place: title, text, courses, under, book and page, pictures and pin, with Save and Delete note. Clicking another note’s dot shows that note in the panel. The close button or Esc closes it (in a field, the first Esc leaves the field). It is there in Full window and Fullscreen too. Go to card leaves the graph and shows the note’s card instead.
  • New notes from the graph. New note beside Fit, the N key, or a double-click on empty space opens the panel with an empty editor. Add note in a dot’s bubble starts it already under that note, about that book, or in that course. Nothing is saved until you press Add note; the new note’s dot is then selected and brought to the middle.
  • Full window (next to Fullscreen) makes the graph fill this browser window, without taking over the screen. It is for people who keep Tracker in a smaller window beside other things. The tick boxes and Fit come along, Esc leaves it, and Open note leaves it before showing the note. It works together with Fullscreen.
  • Fullscreen (when your browser allows it) gives the graph the whole screen. Esc leaves it.
  • The picture stays as you left it. Tracker remembers where each dot is (the same in every browser you use), so the graph looks the same every time you open it, and a dot you move stays where it comes to rest. A new note appears next to what it is connected to. A dot is never left sitting on a line it is not part of: it is moved just off it. Changing how hard the lines pull (Customise) lays everything out again.
  • Four kinds of line for being under something. A dashed line runs from a book to the course it is under, from a note to the book it is about, and from a note to each note it is under. It pulls the lower dot close, so what is under something gathers around it, and it has a look of its own by how far down it is, so that you can tell the levels apart. A primary note is one about a book (or a note with nothing above it), a secondary note is under a primary note, and a tertiary note is under a secondary note, or anywhere further down. The four lines, from the top: subfield and book (dashed, in the course’s colour), book and primary note (heavy dashed, in the theme’s accent colour), primary and secondary note (dashed, the accent half faded) and secondary and tertiary note (thin dashed, grey). A dashed line is always the hierarchy: a [[link]] is a dotted line in the colour of a course the two notes share (or else the first note’s course; orange when neither has one), and a note in a course has a thin solid line in the course’s colour. In a light theme these automatic colours are darkened as much as needed to stay easy to see. None has an arrowhead until you switch it on in Customise. A note has a line to a course only for the courses you gave it yourself; it reaches the others through what is above it. If a [[link]] also joins two notes, they share that one line. A book that is only connected to its course has a plain thin line in the course’s colour instead. Arrowheads, pointing at the one above, can be switched on in Customise.
  • Put under... (next to Connect to... when a note or a book is selected) puts it under the note, book or course you click next: a note goes under a note or a book, a book goes under a course. The shortcut: hold Alt and drag the note or book onto what it goes under (for a book and a course, Connect to... and Shift-drag make a normal connection). See Shortcuts for one for each kind of line. Select an under line and press Disconnect to take a note out from under another, or a book out of its course.
  • Shortcuts. Hold keys while you drag from one dot onto another to choose the kind of connection: Shift makes a normal connection (two notes get a [[link]], a note joins a course, a note is about a book), Shift+1 puts a book under a course, Shift+2 a note under a book, Shift+3 or Shift+4 the dragged note under the other note (the message says whether it became a secondary or a tertiary note), Shift+5 adds a note to a course, and Alt puts it under whatever it is dropped on. For the book and course shortcuts either dot can be the one dragged. If the two dots do not fit the keys, nothing changes and a short line says what the keys join. In the graph, N starts a new note and F fits the picture. Every shortcut can be changed in each tracker’s Settings, Shortcuts (press Change, then the new keys; Esc cancels; a key that is already used is refused). Kept in this browser.
  • Change a connection’s type. Click the line between two notes and its bubble shows Type: Link (a [[link]] in the text) or Under (the first note under the second). Switching from a link takes the [[link]] out of the text (the words stay) and puts the note under the other; switching to a link takes it out from under and adds the [[link]] to its Related line. Swap turns an under line round, so the note that was above goes under. For a book and its course the choice is Under or Normal. A comment on the line moves with it, the line stays selected, and one Undo puts everything back. If the new connection cannot be made (for example it would put a note under itself), nothing changes.
  • A connection can carry a short comment that keeps its history, so you can write why two notes belong together. The comment is written along the line, tilted with it and cut with “...” to fit between the two dots; select the line to read it in full. A changed or removed comment can be brought back with Restore.
  • In any tracker’s Settings, Notes graph, you can choose the shape of each kind of dot and how the graph moves (it follows your computer’s “reduce motion” setting by default).
  • Customise, in the same place, starts closed and is optional. For every kind of line, listed from the most important (it pulls hardest) to the least: subfield and book, book and primary, primary and secondary, secondary and tertiary, links, note and subfield, and suggestions, you can set its style (solid, dashed or dotted), width, colour, opacity and strength (how hard it pulls its two dots together), and for the four lines for being under something whether they have an arrowhead (off to begin with); for every kind of dot its fill colour and opacity and its border colour and width; and the general pull: Spread (how hard dots push apart) and Centre (how hard everything is pulled to the middle). Background sets the picture’s background: automatically it is pitch black in a dark theme (so the dots and lines stand out from it) and the theme’s own in a light one. Automatic keeps the colour the graph picks itself, such as a course’s colour. A line still lights up when its dot is selected and fades when another is. Back to the defaults brings back the usual look. The choices are kept in this browser and used in every tracker.

11. The Library: books

Each tracker’s Library tab has two parts: Books, described here, and Writing. The Archive link in the sidebar (under the trackers) is the shared Archive: every book of every tracker, and books you keep in no tracker (see The shared Archive below).

Adding a text

Press Add. In the box, type:

  • an ISBN: finds that one book (Open Library first, then Google Books for missing details);
  • a DOI: finds that article or chapter on Crossref;
  • words: searches Open Library for books and Crossref for articles and chapters, and also ISBNdb and Google Books if you saved their keys (see below).

Pick a result, check and correct the details (title, authors, year, publisher, pages, edition...) and save. If you choose a course for the book, Tracker asks whether to put the book under that course or to make it a normal connection (see Books under courses, notes under books). Enter it by hand instead skips the search entirely. When a book comes from a search, Tracker also keeps its cover picture.

Dropping files. Drag PDF or EPUB files onto the Library and each becomes a book with its file attached. Tracker reads from the file what it can: the title and authors, the number of pages, and a cover (the first page of a PDF, the cover picture of an EPUB). If the file names its ISBN, Tracker also looks the book up (as Search does) to fill in the publisher, year, edition and the shop cover; Look up dropped books in App settings, Book search, turns that off.

Folders and books under books

  • Folders. The Folders panel beside the list holds your own folders, for books and documents alike: New folder, and on a folder New folder inside, rename and delete. Click a folder to show what is in it (and in the folders inside it); All books and Not in a folder are at the top. Put a book in a folder by dragging it onto the folder, or with Folder on the book. Deleting a folder keeps what was in it: it moves up to the folder above (or out of any folder).
  • Books under books. Under on a book puts it under another book, for a chapter, a volume of a series or an article in a collection. It is then listed indented under that book, at any depth. A book cannot go under itself or a book under it.

The shared Archive

The Archive in the sidebar keeps books you want to keep without a tracker: a library of your documents and books, not for study. Drop PDF or EPUB files on it, or Add a book by its ISBN or title, as in a Library. They can be read in the same reader, with highlights, text boxes and drawings (notes, the timer and Ask belong to a tracker). The shared Archive has folders of its own and books under books. It also lists every tracker’s books, under In trackers. Move into a tracker on a book in no tracker moves it there, with its file and highlights, when you want to take notes on it or log your reading. Take it out of the tracker does the opposite for a book that has no notes, sessions, flashcards or citations yet.

Reading a text

  • Status: To read, Reading, Paused, Read. Starting to read and finishing are dated for you.
  • Now on page: type the page you are on. Tracker shows the percent done, pages per hour from your timed sessions, and the time left (“about 4 h left”). Settings, Books (in any tracker) can also add a guess of how many weeks that is at your recent pace; it is off by default because it is only a rough guess, and the hours are useful on their own.
  • Pick the text in the timer and the session counts towards it (“4h in 3 sessions, last today”).
  • Notes about a text show how many there are, and can name the page they are about. 3 notes opens the Notes tab showing only them, and the time read (4h in 3 sessions) opens the Log showing only them. Cited in lists the documents that cite the book, and the course chip opens the course’s page.
  • Filter by course, search with Search the library, and use Covers to show or hide the pictures.
  • A PDF or EPUB of the book: edit the book and choose Attach a PDF or EPUB (up to 500 MB). Tracker keeps its own copy in a books folder next to your data and never changes your file. The book then has a Read button (see Reading). Replace and Remove are in the same place; removing the file keeps the book, its notes and its highlights. Deleting the book deletes Tracker’s copy of the file. A book whose file is not on this computer (after moving to a new one, for example) says so; attach the file again and it is back, with its highlights.

Citations and sharing

Import and export (below the list) copies your citations as BibTeX or RIS for a reference manager or paper, and reads those formats back in.

Better search with keys (optional)

Open Library and Crossref are free but do not know every edition. In App settings, Book search, you can add an ISBNdb key (a paid service with a short free trial, with a large catalogue in many languages) and/or a Google Books key (free, with limits; you create it in Google Cloud Console). A key is checked when you save it, kept only on your computer, never shown again, and sent only to its own service.

12. Reading

A book with a PDF or EPUB attached (see The Library) opens in Tracker with Read. The book, its notes and its highlights stay on your computer, and the reader asks nothing of any other site: everything it shows comes from Tracker itself.

Reading a PDF

  • The bar along the top has the way back (Books, or Archive for a book in no tracker), Contents (when the PDF has a contents list), Go to notes and the title on the left; the page in the middle (the page box shows the page you are on, of how many: type another and press Enter, or use the arrows), the zoom with its size in percent, and search; and on the right the tools: Text, Pen, the timer, More (…) with Save with notes, Read another book beside, Share pages, Dim the pages, Text boxes beside the page and Page numbers do not match?, and the button that shows or hides the side panel (notes and Ask). Page Down and Page Up go a page on or back.
  • Search in the book (or press /) finds words on every page and marks them; Enter goes to the next one. A word broken over two lines is found too.
  • The minus and plus buttons zoom; the size between them fits the width of the window again (the width most pages have, so a cover or a foldout of another size does not decide it). Dim the pages (under More) makes white pages softer on a dark theme.
  • Page numbers. The reader shows the page number printed in the book (“p. 45”), not the count of PDF pages, so your notes and citations match the paper book. Most PDFs say their own numbers. If one does not, go to the page printed as 1 and choose Page numbers do not match? under More to set them. The book’s Now on page in the Library follows the page you read, forward only: going back to the contents or an earlier chapter does not undo your progress (change it by hand in the Library if you need to).
  • Close the book and open it again: it opens where you were. A file you open for the first time opens at the page the book is at (the progress you kept by hand, for example on paper).
  • A scanned book (pictures of pages) can be read, but its words cannot be selected, searched or asked about; the reader says so. A PDF locked with a password asks for it; Tracker does not keep the password.

Reading an EPUB

An EPUB opens in the same reader, one chapter at a time, with the same bar (the chapter and how far in, with arrows for the chapter before and after, in the middle), notes, highlights and questions. Text size and page colour (the Aa button) sets the size of the letters, the space between lines, the margins, and the page colour (your theme’s, or sepia). A link to another chapter goes there; a link to a web page asks before it opens. The book’s own scripts never run. A book locked by the shop that sold it (DRM) cannot be opened here, and the reader says so.

Notes and highlights

  • The Notes panel beside the book is for writing while you read. What you type there becomes an ordinary note about this book and the page you are on, saved as you type. New note (or N) starts another, and the × in the box clears it. A quote goes into the note being written; when the box holds a note you opened to change, a quote starts a new note instead.
  • Select words on the page: Quote puts them in your note with their page, Highlight marks them in one of eight colours (yellow, green, blue, pink, orange, purple, red, teal), and Ask asks the AI about them (below). Highlighting does not open the Notes panel; Write a note in the highlight’s menu does. The + after the colours adds a colour of your own (up to eight, the same in every browser you use); it is used like the others, in Save with notes too. Lines that are close together are highlighted one by one, without marking the space between them twice.
  • Click a highlight to change its colour, open or write its note, Make a flashcard of it (the highlighted words are the answer), or remove it; removing it keeps its note. Highlights stay on their words at any zoom or text size, and are kept in Tracker, never written into your file.
  • In a PDF, Text writes on the page itself, in a margin or any empty space: press it, click where the text goes and type (leaving the box or Ctrl+Enter keeps it, Esc drops it). Drag a text box to move it, drag its right edge to make it wider, and double-click it to change the text. Opening another box while you write keeps what you wrote in the first. A text box can also go beside the page, in the dark space to its left or right: click there with Text on, or drag a box there. If you would rather not, turn off Text boxes beside the page under More: new and moved boxes then stay on the page, and ones already beside it stay where they are (kept in this browser). New text boxes start at 8 pt. While you write, the small bar over the box has Raise and Lower (Ctrl+Up, Ctrl+Down): they turn the letter before the cursor (or the selected letters) into a raised or lowered one, as in kʲ or x₂, for the letters and numbers that have one (with the IPA’s). Click a box for its colour, its text size, Make it a note (it asks for the note’s title and where it goes, on its own or under a note you find by typing; then it becomes an ordinary note about the book at that page, and the box shows the note: its title on the first line, then its text, with numbered and bulleted lines kept; the Notes panel stays as it was) and Delete text box, which can be undone for a few seconds and keeps the note. A click anywhere else closes its menu. Lock text boxes under More keeps every box where it is: dragging one does nothing, a finger on a box scrolls the page (useful on a phone), and a click or tap still opens its menu. It is remembered in each browser, so the boxes can be locked on the phone and movable on the computer.
  • In a PDF, Pen writes or draws on the page by hand (with the mouse, a pen or a finger): choose the ink (black, blue, red or green) and the thickness, and Undo the last line takes lines back one by one. Done (or Esc) puts the pen down. Click a line later to change its ink or thickness or to delete it.
  • Go to notes (next to Contents; under More on a phone) opens the Notes tab; the Library tab brings you back to the book, at the page where you were.
  • Read another book beside (under More; in an EPUB, the button with two books next to Aa) opens a second PDF or EPUB of the same tracker beside the one you read, to read two texts on one topic side by side. Each keeps its own page. In the second book, select words to Highlight them there (in any colour) or Quote in my note: the quotation goes into the note you are writing, followed by the other book’s title and page, and the note stays about the first book. The buttons on its bar put it on the other side, choose another book or close it. Which book is beside, and on which side, is remembered for each book. An EPUB beside is read chapter by chapter. On a phone-sized screen there is no room for two, so the second book is not shown there.
  • Open in its own window (the arrow out of a box, on the bar of the book beside) moves that book into a window of its own, its reader at the page it was on, to put on another screen. The book beside closes in the first window. Writing has the same button (see Writing). If nothing opens, your browser blocked the new window: allow pop-ups for Tracker.
  • Save with notes (a PDF) saves a copy of the book with your highlights (and the notes written about them, as their comments), text boxes and drawings written into the file as ordinary PDF annotations, so whoever you share it with sees them in Adobe Acrobat, Edge, Chrome, Brave, Firefox or Preview. Tracker's own copy of the book is not changed, so your highlights stay yours to change. A page with a text box beside it is made wider in the copy, with white paper on both sides, so that the box is on the page. The text of a text box is written with the Noto Sans font, which has Turkish, the IPA, Greek and Cyrillic; Chinese, Japanese or Korean in a text box shows as empty boxes on the page, though the full text is in the annotation's comment. A PDF locked with a password cannot be written into.
  • Share pages (under More) saves or shares part of a PDF: This page, or Pages from one to another, as a PDF, or the one page as a PNG picture, with or without your highlights, notes, text boxes and drawings. Share... opens your computer’s own way to share when it has one.
  • The Notes panel lists the book’s notes by page and, to begin with, Follows the page: the last page first, kept scrolled to the notes of the page you are reading (it waits while the pointer is on the list). The button above the list switches to First page first or Last page first, which stay put (Tracker remembers which). Quotations are shown as quotations. Click one to go to its page, with its passage lit up. Notes under a note about the book that have no book of their own are listed too. They are ordinary notes: they are also in the Notes tab and the graph, as notes about the book. Delete note (point at a note) deletes it; Undo brings it back for a few seconds. Search the notes above the list keeps only the notes and highlights with every word you type (in any case, with or without accents); Esc empties it.
  • Whole note (under the note being written) opens the whole note: its subfields, pictures, and Under, the notes it is put under, which is its place in the graph. A note written in the reader goes on its own, linked to the book, unless you choose otherwise with the button left of New note: it shows where new notes go (On its own, or Under a note), and a click lets you find a note to put them under. The choice is remembered for the book, and a new note you are writing moves there too.

Asking about what you read

The Ask tab (with the AI tab turned on, see The AI tab) answers questions about the words you selected or the page you are on, with quick ones such as Explain this or Translate to English. It says which AI answers and where it runs. Only your question and that passage are sent, and only when you ask. Prepare this book for questions reads the whole book into the search index once; then you can ask the whole book and your notes, and the answer’s citations open the reader at the right page. Conversations are saved with the book and are also in the AI tab.

The timer

Opening a book does not start the timer. Start the timer in the reader starts it for this book (choose the course and activity); Started earlier? starts it counting from 5 minutes to an hour ago. While it runs, the time shows on the bar: click it to move the start back. Stop saves an ordinary session, as on the Dashboard, and asks for a note for the Log (optional), already filled in with the pages you read (“pp. 12-30”; for an EPUB, the chapters). The notes you wrote in the reader while it ran belong to that session.

13. Writing

Each tracker’s Library tab has two parts: Books, with the books and articles you read, and Writing, with what you write yourself: essays, articles, chapters, a talk. A document is saved as you type, in your own data on your computer, and nothing in it is sent anywhere. The Library tab remembers where you were: go to Notes or the graph and come back, and the document you were writing (with its panel or its book's PDF open at the same page) or the book you were reading is there again.

Your documents

  • New document starts one. The list shows the newest first, with the number of words and when it was last changed. Search with Search your documents and filter by course.
  • Import opens a Word file (.docx) or a plain text file as a new document, to go on writing it here: headings, lists, tables, quotations, bold and the rest, links, pictures and footnotes come with it. A first heading (or Word’s Title) becomes the document’s title. Your file is not changed or kept. What Word has and Tracker does not (fonts of single words, comments, tracked changes, drawings) is left out, and Tracker says when something was. An old .doc file must be saved as .docx in Word first.
  • Give a document a course under Page, so it shows under that course.
  • Folders: the same folders as the Library’s (see Folders). Drag a document onto a folder, or choose its Folder in the list.
  • Start the timer in a document times its writing: it starts with the document’s course and a writing activity, has Started earlier?, and the time spent shows next to it (“3h 20m writing”). The sessions are in the Log, with a link back to the document.
  • The bin button in a document moves it to the Bin (it asks first). In the Bin, Take out brings it back and Delete for good deletes it and its earlier versions, after asking.

Writing

  • The toolbar: undo and redo, Text or Heading 1 to 4, the font and text size of the selected words (the first choice is the document's own), bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, superscript and subscript, lining up (left, centre, right, or justified with both edges straight), bulleted and numbered lists, quotation, link, picture and table (with buttons to add and remove rows and columns while you are in it).
  • IPA opens the IPA symbols: consonants, vowels, length, stress and tone, and the diacritics (click a diacritic after the letter it goes on).
  • Page sets the whole document out: its citation style and course, its font, text size, line spacing (single, 1.15, 1.5, double, or any number), the space after paragraphs, the page margins in centimetres, Page numbers (top or bottom, left, centre or right, and if you like none on the first page), and Number the headings (Heading 1 is 1., 2., a Heading 2 under it 1.1, 1.2, a Heading 3 1.1.1). The PDF, Word and OpenDocument files follow them. A new document starts with the settings of the last one you wrote. A font that is not on this computer says so; Word or LibreOffice on another computer use their own in its place.
  • Paper shows the document on white A4 pages with its margins, as it prints, so you see where each page ends: a paragraph goes on at the top of the next page, a long table goes on row by row, the footnotes are at the foot of their page and the page numbers in the margin. The pages are drawn smaller when the window is narrow. Press it again for one long column. The choice is this computer's, not the document's.
  • Book quotes a book straight from its own PDF or EPUB, without a note first: choose a book of your Library that has its file, find words in it (or go to a page with the arrows or by its number), select words on the page shown, and press Quote in the text (in quotation marks) or As a block quotation. They go where the cursor is, with the citation of that page. For a PDF, Show the PDF beside the document shows the book itself, page by page, next to your document, with your highlights: select words on its page and choose Quote in the text or As a block quotation; Highlight too also keeps the quoted words as a highlight in the book (it stays on until you turn it off). Text view goes back to the panel. Open in its own window (on the PDF’s bar, and at the top of the panel) opens the book in the reader in a window of its own, for a second screen.
  • Notes opens your notes beside the document. Quote it here puts a note in as a quotation, with its citation when the note is about a book and page; Open at p. 45 shows the book there, and Open the note opens it in the Notes tab.
  • Versions: while you write, Tracker keeps an earlier version now and then (at most one every ten minutes, the newest 50). Look shows one; Put this version back brings it back, and what was there just before is kept as a version too, so nothing is lost.

Citations and the bibliography

Cite opens your Library: pick a book or article, type the page (“45” or “45-47”) if you like, and press Cite. The citation is written in the style chosen for the document, under Page: APA (7th edition), Chicago (author-date), MLA (9th edition) or the Unified Style Sheet for Linguistics. Changing the style rewrites every citation at once. The bibliography (References, or Works Cited in MLA) is made from the books you cite and stays under the text. Click a citation to change its page, to remove it, or to Open the book at p. 45 (a PDF opens beside the document at that page; an EPUB in the reader). A new document takes the style you used last.

Footnotes and examples

  • Footnote puts a numbered footnote where the cursor is; type its text, and click its number later to change it. Footnotes are numbered in the order they appear.
  • Example adds a numbered linguistic example: (1), (2) and so on, in the order they appear. Type the sentence, its gloss and a free translation. Write the gloss one item per word, with grammatical labels in capitals (1SG-PST-eat-FV food): each gloss stands under its word, and the labels are shown in small capitals, as the Leipzig Glossing Rules have it. A judgement (*, ?, ??, #, %) goes before the sentence. Add a line makes a set: (1a), (1b). Double-click an example, or press Edit, to change it.

Downloading

  • PDF opens the print window: choose Save as PDF. It prints the pages exactly as the paper view shows them (footnotes at the foot of each page, page numbers), with the fonts on your computer, so every script and every IPA symbol comes out as you see it. In the print window, leave the margins at their default (the pages bring their own).
  • Word (.docx), OpenDocument (.odt) (the open format of LibreOffice and other programs) and Markdown (.md) download a file named after the title. In Word and OpenDocument, footnotes are real footnotes and an example is a table without lines, one word above its gloss. A picture in WebP format is left out of a Word file (Word cannot show it); PNG, JPEG and GIF go in.

14. The AI tab

Optional and off by default. Until you turn it on, the tab shows “The AI tab is off” and nothing is ever sent anywhere.

Turning it on

App settings, AI assistant, Show the AI tab. Then choose an engine:

  • Ollama (recommended for privacy): a free program from ollama.com (Download Ollama, shown while Tracker cannot find it, opens its own download page) that runs language models on your own computer. Install and start it, and Tracker finds it at the address shown (Where Ollama is running; the default is right unless it runs on another computer; if it cannot be reached, Tracker says so and has a check again link). Under Suggested for this computer Tracker recommends models that suit your computer’s memory and graphics card and can ask Ollama to download them, with a progress bar. Choose a Model that answers questions and, optionally, a Model that searches your notes (without one, Tracker matches words only; a multilingual one finds notes by meaning). Test the connection checks that they work. Nothing leaves your computer.
  • An online service that works like OpenAI’s: a Service address (OpenAI and many others use one ending in /v1), a Chat model, a Search model and a Key (Save key, or Remove it again). Use this only if you accept that the text of your notes that the AI needs is sent to that company.

Ask your notes

Type a question. Tracker finds the pieces of your notes and books that match (it reads them in small pieces through a search index; App settings, AI assistant, shows how many pieces are indexed across all your trackers and has Update the search index to bring the index of every tracker up to date), and the model answers only from them, naming its sources as numbered citations you can click to open the note (or, for a book you prepared in the reader, the book at its page). If your notes do not contain the answer it says so. A question about your study time (“how many hours did I spend on Statistics this week?”) is answered from your real totals, not from the model’s arithmetic. Your conversations are listed so you can reopen, rename or delete them.

Keeping an answer

Under an answer, here and in the reader’s Ask tab, Save as a note keeps it as an ordinary note (the question as its title, the sources it cites listed under it; in the reader, about that book), and Make a flashcard makes a card of it.

Flashcards

Flashcards are in the Notes tab (see Flashcards); the Flashcards button here goes there. With the AI on, Make cards from your notes with the AI there picks a note (or the notes of a course or a book) and the model proposes cards you can edit, then save.

What is sent: with Ollama on this computer, nothing leaves it. With another computer or an online service, your questions and the notes needed to answer go there; in the reader, your question and the passage it is about. The Privacy section spells it out.

15. Your phone

Tracker can open on your phone, in the phone’s browser, while it runs on your computer. Your data stays on the computer: the phone shows it and changes it there. It is off by default, there is no account, and nothing goes through the internet or any company.

Turning it on and pairing a phone

  1. In App settings, Your phone, tick Use Tracker from your phone. The first time, Windows asks whether Node.js (Tracker) may use the network: allow it for private networks. A Mac asks whether to accept incoming connections: choose Allow.
  2. Press Pair a phone. Tracker shows a QR code and a one-time code. The code works once, for ten minutes, and ends after five wrong tries.
  3. With the phone on the same Wi-Fi, scan the QR code with its camera, open the address and press Pair this phone. (Or open the address shown and type the code.) Tracker opens on the phone.
  4. To keep it like an app: on an iPhone, Share, Add to Home Screen; on Android, the browser’s menu, Add to home screen.

Your phones are listed under Paired phones; the bin button removes one, and it can no longer open Tracker until it is paired again. Unticking the box closes the way in at once; the phones stay paired for when you tick it again. This is part of the installed Tracker (started from its icon).

On the phone

The Dashboard and the timer, quick notes, flashcards and reading are made for a phone’s screen. In the reader, a page fits the width, you turn pages by scrolling (the page box still takes a page number), the notes open over the page with the panel button and close with the × (or when you go to a note’s page), and words selected with a finger get the same menu as with a mouse. Writing and the graph are better on the computer.

What follows you from browser to browser

Some choices follow you to every browser you use Tracker in, the phone included, within a few seconds: the timer while it runs (start it on the computer, stop it on the phone), the theme and your changes to it, the notes graph’s shapes, look, options and where its dots are, your own highlight colours, your shortcuts, the order of the reader’s notes, break reminders, Show cover pictures, the weeks estimate, Look up dropped books, the AI tab being on, and how Stats splits its bars. What belongs to one screen stays in its own browser: the sidebar open or closed, the reader’s zoom and panels, the paper view in Writing and the like. The choices that follow you are kept with your data on the computer, so the complete copy holds them too.

Away from home: Tailscale

Tailscale is a free app, made by another company, that links your own devices into a private, encrypted network from anywhere. Install it on the computer and on the phone (Download Tailscale in App settings, Your phone, opens its own download page), sign in with the same account on both, and pair the phone with the Tailscale address (it starts with 100.), chosen under Address in the code. Tracker is not part of Tailscale and sends it nothing.

Keeping it safe

  • Only paired phones get in. Any other device gets the pairing page and nothing else, and a request from another website is refused, as on the computer.
  • A phone cannot quit Tracker, install updates, or pair and remove phones: those stay on the computer.
  • On your own Wi-Fi the connection is not encrypted. On a shared network (a café, a library, a school), use Tailscale, which encrypts it.
  • Lost a phone? Remove it in App settings, Your phone.
  • The computer has to be on, with Tracker running.

16. Themes and looks

Settings, Theme (in any tracker) offers six themes: Midnight (the default dark one), Black, Light, Forest, Rose and Frutiger Aero (a glassy, see-through style), plus Custom.

  • Customize (under the themes) changes any theme’s colours one by one, the font (the theme’s own, a system font, or one you name), the text size and how round the corners are. Everything shows on the page at once. A small button puts any colour back; Reset restores the theme.
  • If the colours you pick make some text hard to read, a warning says which text and how far off it is. Every built-in theme passes the standard readability rule (WCAG AA).
  • Custom starts from the colours of any theme you pick and lets you build your own.
  • Minimise what you do not use. Every section of both settings pages has a title you can click to minimise it (and again to open it). The choice is remembered in this browser, and a link that points at a minimised section opens it.
  • Theme and look apply to every tracker and are the same in every browser you use, your phone too. Show cover pictures (Settings, Books) hides or shows book covers everywhere.

17. Backups, exports, moving

Where your data is

Everything (sessions, notes, books, highlights, pictures and covers) is in one file, tracker.db, in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Tracker\data (paste that into the File Explorer address bar; on a Mac, ~/Library/Application Support/Tracker/data). Saved copies are in its backups folder, and the PDF and EPUB files you attached to books in its books folder. App settings, Privacy lists all the places for your computer.

Saved copies

  • Tracker saves one copy automatically each day and keeps the last 14. Back up now makes one on request; those are kept until you remove them.
  • Tracker also saves a copy by itself before something risky, and labels it in the list: Before a restore, Before an update (when a new version has to change the database) and Before a delete (deleting a tracker). The list also shows Automatic, Manual, Export file and Other file entries.
  • Restore... on a saved copy shows what is in it (trackers, sessions, hours, notes, books) next to what you have now, and asks you to confirm. Everything you have now is replaced by the copy, including its settings; keys you saved on this computer are kept unless the copy has its own. Your current data is saved first as Before a restore, so a restore can be undone. A copy from an older version is brought up to date; one from a newer version is refused (update Tracker first).
  • Those copies live on the same computer as your data. Now and then copy the folder to a USB stick or cloud drive.
  • Book files are not in the saved copies (so the daily copies stay small). Notes, highlights and which file belongs to which book are. After restoring a copy on a computer without the files, each book asks for its file again.

Importing a file

Import from a file..., next to Back up now, lets you pick a complete copy (.db) or a readable-data zip from anywhere on your computer, for example one made on another computer or kept on a USB stick. Tracker keeps a copy of it in the list of saved copies, shows what is in it next to what you have now, and replaces nothing until you confirm. A file that does not start like one of Tracker’s own files is refused, and a file that is a database but not Tracker’s is explained in the preview.

Your documents in Writing, and their earlier versions, are part of your data: the daily copies, the complete copy and the readable data hold them.

All three downloads cover all your trackers. Your saved keys (AI, book search) are left out of the complete copy and the zip on purpose, so the files are safe in a cloud drive.

Moving to another computer

Install Tracker there, make a complete copy or a zip here (with Include my books if you read books in Tracker), and on the new computer press Import from a file... and pick it (see below). Or, with Tracker closed on both, copy the whole %LOCALAPPDATA%\Tracker folder (~/Library/Application Support/Tracker on a Mac).

If the data file is damaged

After a crash, a power cut or a disk problem the file can become unreadable. Tracker then still starts, says so in plain words, and lists the healthy saved copies it found with what each holds. Use this copy puts one in place; the damaged file is not deleted but moved to a damaged folder next to your data. Anything you added after that copy was made is not in it.

18. Updates

  • App settings, About also has a link to the terms of use, the contact address and the GitHub issues page. They are links you click; Tracker sends nothing by itself.
  • Check now asks whether a newer version exists. It is the only thing that contacts anyone unless you turn the next option on.
  • Check for updates once a day (off by default) does the same by itself, once a day.
  • When a newer version exists, Update now downloads it from GitHub (about 45 MB), checks that it is the file signed for Tracker, saves a copy of your data, closes Tracker, installs it and opens Tracker again; the page reloads by itself. Your data stays as it is. One press does all of it: the progress shows while it downloads.
  • Install updates by itself (off by default) downloads a newer version in the background and installs it the next time Tracker starts. It also turns on the daily check. The About page shows the download as it goes and then Install now, to install it at once instead; Update now pressed while it downloads installs it as soon as it is ready.
  • Only an update signed with Tracker’s own key is ever installed automatically, and the program that runs the installer checks that signature (and the file’s checksum) again before it runs anything. An update that is not signed is announced with a link, and you run it yourself.
  • You can always update by hand: download the newest Tracker-Setup.exe and run it. It closes Tracker, installs over it and keeps your data. On a Mac: quit Tracker and drag the Tracker from the newest Tracker-Mac.dmg onto the Applications folder. (On a Mac, Update now works only when Tracker is in a folder you can change, such as Applications.) (Versions before 0.3.0 cannot update themselves; install 0.3.0 or later by hand once.)
  • Each release lists a SHA-256 checksum. In PowerShell, Get-FileHash .\Tracker-Setup.exe should show the same number; on a Mac, shasum -a 256 Tracker-Mac.dmg in Terminal.

20. Keyboard and accessibility

  • Everything can be done with the keyboard: Tab and Shift+Tab move between controls, Enter or Space press them, and the focus is always visible.
  • Windows that open on top (New tracker, adding a book) keep Tab inside them, close with Esc and give the focus back to what opened them.
  • In a list with a grip (courses, activities, notes), focus the grip and press Up or Down to move the item. On touch screens it becomes two small arrow buttons. Trackers in the sidebar are moved with the Move up and Move down buttons in each tracker’s Settings, under This tracker.
  • Search everything: Ctrl+K (Cmd+K on a Mac), / outside the reader, or the magnifying glass in the top bar. Type to find your notes, books, documents and flashcards, or the name of a tab to go there; the arrow keys choose and Enter opens.
  • Ctrl+Enter saves a note. In the reader, Page Down and Page Up turn the page and / searches the book. These and the graph’s shortcuts can be changed in each tracker’s Settings, Shortcuts.
  • Every button and field has a name a screen reader can read, each page has a heading, and error messages are announced when they appear.
  • Text in every built-in theme meets the WCAG AA contrast of 4.5 to 1, and the theme editor warns about colours that do not. Calendar squares are at least 24 pixels wide.
  • The graph’s animation follows your computer’s “reduce motion” setting unless you choose otherwise.

What has not been done: it has been checked with automated tools and by keyboard, but not yet with a real screen reader. If something does not work for you, write to clite.ka@gmail.com or open an issue on GitHub.

21. Troubleshooting

Windows says “Windows protected your PC”

The installer is not signed with a paid code-signing certificate. Choose More info, then Run anyway. To check the file is the real one, compare its SHA-256 (Get-FileHash) with the checksum on the release page.

Nothing opened, or the browser says it cannot connect

Wait a few seconds and reload: Tracker takes a moment to start. Look for its icon near the clock (the little arrow shows hidden icons), or in the menu bar on a Mac; clicking it opens Tracker. Starting Tracker again just opens the running one. If it still fails, open the log (right-click the icon, Show the log folder) and read the last lines.

I cannot find the tray icon

Windows may hide it under the arrow next to the clock. Drag it out to keep it visible. You can always start Tracker from the Start menu. On a Mac with a full menu bar, icons can be hidden (behind the notch on a laptop); opening Tracker from the Applications folder opens the running one.

macOS says Tracker cannot be opened

That is the first-start check, once: see Install on a Mac. If Update now says “Move Tracker into your Applications folder first”, Tracker is running from the disk image or a folder you cannot change: drag it into Applications and start it from there.

“Your data file is damaged”

Choose the newest saved copy listed and press Use this copy. The damaged file is kept in the damaged folder. See Backups. If no copy is listed, close Tracker and look in the backups folder shown on that page; the copies there are ordinary .db files, and someone you trust can help you look.

“The disk is full”, or a save fails

Free some space (empty the Recycle Bin, remove files you do not need) and try again. What you saved earlier is safe, and a running timer keeps running until a save works.

“Tracker cannot write to its data” or “Your data is busy”

A security or sync program may be holding the data folder. Wait a few seconds and retry; if it keeps happening, allow Tracker to use %LOCALAPPDATA%\Tracker (~/Library/Application Support/Tracker on a Mac).

A book search finds nothing, or says a service did not answer

Check your internet connection. Open Library does not know every edition; add an ISBNdb or Google Books key in App settings, Book search (see The Library), or choose Enter it by hand instead. A search for an article needs its DOI or title words (Crossref).

Update now does not work

It says why. If it fails to download, check your connection and try again; you can always run the newest Tracker-Setup.exe by hand. Version 0.3.0 could give up on computers where looking up GitHub’s address is slow; 0.3.1 fixed that, so update by hand to 0.3.1 or later once.

A book says its file is not on this computer

That happens after restoring a copy without the books, or when the file in the books folder was deleted. Edit the book and choose Attach it again with the same PDF or EPUB; its notes and highlights are still there. Next time, tick Include my books in the complete copy (see Backups) to take the files with you.

The phone cannot open Tracker

Check that Tracker is running on the computer and Use Tracker from your phone is on (App settings, Your phone says Ready for phones), and that the phone is on the same Wi-Fi (or Tailscale is on on both). A guest Wi-Fi often keeps devices apart: use the main one. On Windows, the first time, allow Node.js (Tracker) on private networks; if you chose otherwise, allow it in Windows Security, Firewall, Allow an app through firewall. If the phone shows the pairing page, it is not paired (or was removed): pair it again.

The AI tab says to choose a model, or cannot reach Ollama

Install Ollama from ollama.com and make sure it is running (its icon is near the clock), then open App settings, AI assistant, and press check again. Choose a model that answers questions (and, optionally, one that searches your notes); the suggestions listed for your computer can be downloaded from there. Test the connection tells you which part fails.

“Tracker cannot find its data file”

The data file is not where Tracker expects it, or cannot be opened. Close Tracker and open it again. If the file is really gone, Tracker starts fresh, and your saved copies are still in the backups folder: open App settings, Data and backups, and restore one.

I started a timer on one computer and cannot see it on another

The running timer lives in the browser it was started in. Tracker data does not sync between computers; save the session where you started it.

Where is the log, and what do I send when I ask for help?

Right-click the Tracker icon and choose Show the log folder (or see App settings, About). It is a plain text file named tracker.log. A failed save can leave a piece of what you were saving in it, so read it before you send it to anyone (for example to clite.ka@gmail.com), and send only what you are comfortable with.

How do I remove all my data?

Uninstall Tracker and answer Yes when it asks about your data, or delete the %LOCALAPPDATA%\Tracker folder (on a Mac, ~/Library/Application Support/Tracker) with Tracker closed. This cannot be undone, so make a complete copy first if you might want it.

22. Questions

Is it free? Is it open source?

It is free to download and use. It is not open source: the program’s code is not published. The terms of use say what you may do with it.

Does it work without the internet?

Yes. The timer, log, notes, goals, stats, backups, reading your books and the AI tab with Ollama on your computer all work offline. Only book lookups, covers, updates and an online AI service need a connection.

Can two people share a tracker?

No. Tracker is a private program for one person on one computer. Your data is not shared or synced.

Can I use it on my phone?

Yes, while Tracker runs on your computer: turn on Use Tracker from your phone in App settings and pair the phone (see Your phone). The phone opens Tracker on the computer; there is no separate phone app, and your data stays on the computer.

What happens to my data if I update?

It stays. A newer version installs over the old one, brings the data file up to date if needed, and a copy is saved first.

How much data can it hold?

It is built for years of daily use by one person; there is no fixed limit. Pictures in notes make the data file bigger, so the backups folder grows with them (daily copies are limited to 14). The PDF and EPUB files of your books are kept once, in the books folder, and are not copied into the backups.

Does it work on a Mac or Linux?

On a Mac, yes: macOS 13.5 or newer, Apple Silicon and Intel (Install on a Mac). On Linux, not yet.

Who made it, and how do I report a problem or suggest something?

Kerem Ates makes it. Write to clite.ka@gmail.com, or open an issue on GitHub if you have a GitHub account. Please read your log first (see Troubleshooting) and do not send anything private.
